The Water Cycle

The water cycle describes how water moves continuously between the atmosphere, land, and oceans. It has no true starting point, since the process repeats endlessly and links every part of the planet's water supply.

Main Stages

The cycle is usually broken down into a few key stages that occur again and again. Each stage transforms water between its liquid, vapor, and solid states.

Evaporation

Heat from the sun causes water in oceans, lakes, and rivers to turn into vapor and rise into the atmosphere. Plants also release water vapor through a related process called transpiration.

Condensation

As water vapor rises and cools, it condenses into tiny droplets that form clouds. This stage makes the water in the atmosphere visible again.

Precipitation

When cloud droplets combine and grow heavy enough, they fall back to the earth as rain, snow, or hail. Precipitation returns water to the land and oceans, completing part of the cycle.

Why It Matters

The water cycle supplies fresh water to ecosystems, agriculture, and human communities around the world. Understanding it helps explain weather patterns, climate, and the availability of drinking water.

Groundwater and Runoff

Some precipitation soaks into the soil and replenishes underground water reserves called aquifers. The rest flows over the surface as runoff, eventually returning to streams, rivers, and oceans.
